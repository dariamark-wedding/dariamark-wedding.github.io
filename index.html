Wedding details

Location
Continental Citygolf Club
Perényi út 6, 1037 Budapest, Hungary
View on Google Maps ↗

Our wedding will take place at the Continental Citygolf Club, located within the city of Budapest. Both the ceremony and the reception will be held here, so once you arrive, you can simply relax and enjoy the day with us - there will be no need to travel between locations.

The venue is easily accessible by Uber, Bolt, or taxi from anywhere in Budapest. A ride from the city centre typically takes around 20 minutes and costs approximately CHF 10–15, depending on traffic. Taxis are readily available throughout the city, day and night, so getting to and from the venue is quick and hassle-free. Note that all licensed Taxis in Budapest are painted yellow and display the government-mandated tariffs on their sides. Uber and Bolt fall under the same tariff.

Budapest also has an excellent public transport network, and the venue is well-connected. The nearest bus stop is Perényi út, served by buses 160, 260 and 260A, while the closest tram stop is Váradi utca, served by trams 17, 19 and 41. From either stop, it's just a short walk to the venue. For buying tickets, use either the ticket machines in larger stations or the BudapestGO app.

What to wear

We don't have a strict dress code, we simply want everyone to feel comfortable and celebrate this special day with us. However, note that according to Hungarian tradition, white and red are reserved for the bride.

Our ceremony will (weather permitting) take place outdoors, on the golf course, followed by an indoor reception and dinner. If you plan to wear heels, we recommend bringing heel protectors, as you'll be walking on grass (and you might sink in the grass). Early October in Budapest is usually mild, with daytime temperatures highs of around 17–20°C and evenings cooling to 9–12°C. We recommend bringing a light jacket or shawl for the ceremony and evening time, just in case.

Plan your stay

Where to stay

Our venue is conveniently located in the city, so getting there is easy from almost anywhere in Budapest. Whether you stay in Buda or Pest, you can expect the journey to take around 20 minutes by car.

For reference, a ride from St. Stephen's Basilica, right in the heart of the city, takes approximately 20 minutes, depending on traffic.

For sightseeing, we recommend staying in one of the central districts, such as District V (Belváros–Lipótváros), District VI (Terézváros), District VII (Erzsébetváros), District VIII (Józsefváros), or District IX (Ferencváros). These neighborhoods are home to many of Budapest's most famous landmarks, restaurants, cafés and bars, making them the perfect base for your visit. Make sure to stay within the ring road though (where the tram 4-6 drives). Our personal recommendation would be any of Districts V, VIII, or IX close to the National Museum of Hungary/Kálvin tér.

Traveling to Budapest

Flying (our recommendation)

For most of our Swiss guests, the easiest option is to fly from Basel with Wizz Air. If booked in advance, return tickets are often available from around CHF 50–70, and the direct flight takes just 1 hour 45 minutes.

By train

If you prefer a more relaxed journey, the direct daytime train from Zürich takes around 10–11 hours. It's comfortable, scenic, and tickets usually start from around CHF 55 when booked early.

Night train

A direct overnight train is also available. While it sounds romantic, we honestly only recommend it if you're already a sleeper train enthusiast. The beds are rather compact, and arriving well-rested isn't guaranteed.

Our Budapest favourites

If it's your first time in Budapest, of course, don't miss the city's iconic landmarks, such as the Hungarian Parliament, Fisherman's Bastion & Buda Castle, and St. Stephen's Basilica. They are Budapest's most famous sights for a reason. If you have the chance, take a walk along the Danube at sunset, or hop on Tram 2 for a few stops, which runs right along the Danube and is often listed as one of Europe's most scenic tram rides.

Mark also has prepared his usual intro to Budapest walking path, described in this G-Doc.

Ervin Szabó Library – One of our favorite hidden gems. It looks more like a palace than a library, and it's the perfect place to escape the crowds for some reading or doing a bit of work. You can get a basic library card for just HUF 400 (about CHF 1), which gives you access to the reading rooms and Wi-Fi for a whole year.

If you ask us, the best chimney cake in Budapest is from Molnár's kürtőskalács. It's just a short walk from the Danube and is the perfect place to sit down with a freshly baked kürtőskalács after exploring the city.

Szimpla Kert – Budapest's original ruin bar and probably the city's most iconic nightlife spot. Yes, it's veeeeery touristy, but for a good reason. According to Mark, it was an inspiration for these kind of bars in Berlin (ofc). Grab a drink there if you wish, but definitely do not miss exploring other (ruin) bars in the area.

Some assorted places we have visited recently and enjoyed: Ramen Nikko (great ramen place near the library), Tifliso Restaurant (amazing and authentic Georgian restaurant), In Town Hot Pot (authentic hot pot in Budapest Chinatown), Kőleves kert (nice Hungarian gastro), Gettó Gulyás (great intro to Hungarian kitchen), and Tuk Tuk Bár (very nice cocktails and informative waiters). This is in no way an exhaustive list though, there are plenty of great coffee places, restaurants, brunch places, bars, craft breweries, etc. Rely on your curiosity and google maps :) (and on Robin ofc. when it comes to coffee or fine-dining).
